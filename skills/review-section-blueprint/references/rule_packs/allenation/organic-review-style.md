# Organic review style for allene chemistry

## Voice and information order

Lead with the chemical judgment, then the conditions and evidence that determine it. Prefer specific statements such as “aryl aldehydes remained low-yielding” to labels such as “the method has limitations.”

Useful paragraph orders include:

```text
missing product class -> enabling design -> demonstrated scope -> remaining boundary
method identity -> representative conditions -> outcome distribution -> mechanism evidence
two methods -> shared comparison axes -> tradeoff -> selection consequence
```

## Chemical identity

Keep these terms precise:

- `propargyl`, `propargylic`, `allenyl`, and `allene` describe different structures or positions.
- Name the actual precursor class: alcohol, carbonate, acetate, halide, alkyne, enyne, ketene, or another derivative.
- State allene substitution and functionalization when they define the method's reach.
- Use `SN2′` for reaction topology when supported; describe the detailed pathway separately.
- E/Z notation applies to suitable alkenes, not to axial allene stereochemistry.

## Catalyst and component roles

Separate:

```text
catalyst
ligand or chiral source
promoter or activator
reductant or oxidant
base or acid
stoichiometric coupling partner
solvent and energy input
```

When a component performs different roles in successive stages, state each stage. Report catalyst loading and unusual reagent equivalents when they affect practicality or the mechanistic claim.

## Stereochemistry

- Distinguish axial chirality from central chirality.
- Separate catalyst induction from point-to-axis or axis-to-center chirality transfer.
- Report ee/er, dr/de, regioselectivity, enantiospecificity, and chirality-transfer efficiency as different quantities.
- Attach every value to the correct product, fraction, diastereomer, or recovered starting material.
- Preserve erosion between starting material and product when it changes the method assessment.
- For matched/mismatched cases, identify the substrate and catalyst combination.
- State whether ee was measured directly or inferred through a derivative or optical rotation when that affects confidence.

## Scope

Summarize scope by chemically meaningful classes:

```text
alkyl, aryl, heteroaryl, and conjugated partners
terminal and internal alkynes
primary, secondary, and tertiary propargylic centers
allene substitution pattern
functional-group families
steric and electronic boundary cases
```

Give representative ranges and name the outliers that determine the boundary. A substrate absent from the study remains untested; a reported trace or failed result is a demonstrated failure.

## Mechanism

Map each statement to its evidence:

```text
product distribution
control reaction
isotope or crossover experiment
intermediate trapping or direct observation
kinetic or spectroscopic result
computation
author proposal
review inference
```

Reaction naming and mechanism proof are separate. For example, an SN2′ product topology does not by itself establish a concerted displacement, and a proposed catalytic cycle does not establish every intermediate.

## Major allene method families

### ATA and EATA platforms

Track aldehyde or ketone identity, alkyne class, amine component, copper loading, allene substitution, and racemic or asymmetric control. Separate an initial stoichiometric enabling protocol from later catalytic practicalization. For tandem or one-pot variants, keep the stages and their distinct catalysts visible.

### Propargylic substitution and coupling

Record electrophile class, nucleophile or coupling partner, metal/ligand system, activation mode, product topology, regioselectivity, and stereochemical outcome. Compare carbonates, acetates, phosphates, halides, alcohols, and cyclic carbonates as distinct precursor families.

### Enyne and hydrofunctionalization routes

State enyne substitution, directing group, metal/ligand system, addition topology, product class, competing products, and how ee was assigned. Keep background reactions and substrate-controlled effects separate from catalyst control.

### Ketene, ylide, and isomerization routes

Separate ketene generation from olefination, and catalyst generation from the stereochemistry-forming step. For alkyne-to-allene isomerization, report conversion, product yield, allene/alkyne ratio, and ee separately. Distinguish resonance descriptions from observed intermediates.

### Resolution and desymmetrization

Report conversion, selectivity factor, product ee, and recovered-substrate ee together. Identify whether the process is kinetic resolution, dynamic kinetic resolution, salt resolution, or desymmetrization, and keep its product branches distinct.

## Practical evidence

Treat scale-up, lower loading, simplified operation, safer reagent choice, and improved reproducibility as separate advances. Tie each to the demonstrated protocol and substrate. A practical descriptor is supported by the conditions and operation, not by promotional wording.

## Figures and tables

For reaction schemes, verify structures, bond connectivity, stereochemistry, reagents, conditions, yields, labels, and panel completeness. Comparison tables keep method families and metric definitions visible.

## Language

Replace generic expressions with the measured change:

| Generic phrase | Useful replacement |
|---|---|
| mild conditions | temperature, time, atmosphere, and reagent burden |
| broad scope | demonstrated substrate classes and failures |
| excellent selectivity | value, metric, product, and range |
| efficient | yield/rate/loading/operation that defines efficiency |
| novel | the new bond, precursor, product class, or control strategy |
| challenging | the specific low-reactivity, selectivity, stability, or access problem |

## Paragraph check

The final paragraph identifies the method, preserves the decisive chemical detail, states the evidence level, and gives the comparison or boundary that matters to the review.
