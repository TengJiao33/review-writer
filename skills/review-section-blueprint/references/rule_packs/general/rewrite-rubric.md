# Rewrite rubric

Use this rubric after drafting a paragraph or section.

## Evidence

- Every material factual clause has a linked source.
- The source level is adequate for the claim.
- Numbers, units, denominators, conditions, and comparison baselines are preserved.
- Direct results, author explanations, and review inferences are labeled at their actual certainty.
- Citations support the complete clause beside them.

## Synthesis

- The opening sentence gives the paragraph's judgment.
- Evidence is selected for that judgment rather than copied in source order.
- Multiple papers are connected by a named relationship or comparison axis.
- The paragraph ends with a consequence, boundary, or transition.
- Repeated background and paper-by-paper chronology are compressed.

## Scope and comparison

- Generality is described from tested classes and visible failures.
- Best cases are separated from typical performance.
- Compared methods use the same evaluation axis.
- Tradeoffs remain visible.
- Missing evidence leads to narrower wording.

## Style

- Subjects and actions are explicit.
- Sentences carry one main relationship.
- Technical terms are consistent.
- Abstract labels such as “innovation,” “advantage,” and “challenge” are replaced by the specific change or limitation.
- Qualifications appear next to the claim they limit and are stated once.

## Release decision

A paragraph is ready when a reader can answer three questions: What is the judgment? Which evidence supports it? Where does it stop being true?
