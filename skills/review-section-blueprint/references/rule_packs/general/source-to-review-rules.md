# Source-to-review rules

Use these rules to turn checked sources into review prose.

## Evidence first

For each source, identify:

```text
its role in the review
the claim it can support
the source passage, figure, or table
the relevant result or comparison
the evidence boundary
```

Use source text and recorded evidence anchors for scientific facts. Keep author interpretation, reviewer inference, and direct observation distinct.

## Paragraph construction

Build a paragraph around one review judgment:

1. State the method, problem, or comparison.
2. Give the evidence that determines the judgment.
3. Compare it with the relevant baseline or alternative.
4. State the scope, uncertainty, or limitation that changes interpretation.
5. Connect the result to the section argument.

A paper may occupy a whole paragraph when depth is useful. Several papers may share a paragraph when they answer the same comparison.

## Source roles

Useful roles include:

- foundational method or observation
- representative implementation
- strategic extension
- comparison source
- mechanistic evidence
- boundary or negative result
- practical application
- historical or field context

Make the role visible when it explains why the source is included.

## Logical relationships

Choose transitions from the evidence relationship:

```text
gap -> solution
baseline -> improvement
advance -> tradeoff
scope -> boundary
proposal -> supporting or contradicting evidence
platform -> extension
reported result -> reproducibility correction
method -> application
several approaches -> comparative synthesis
```

State both sides of a tradeoff in the same sentence or adjacent sentences. Keep method families separate when their conditions, outcomes, or evidence levels differ.

## Claim calibration

- Report measured quantities with their object, conditions, and source.
- Attach selectivity, rate, yield, accuracy, or other metrics to the correct product, dataset, or system.
- Treat maxima as examples; characterize a method from its demonstrated range and named outliers.
- Scope claims name the tested classes and important failures.
- Mechanism wording reflects the evidence: observed, supported, consistent with, proposed, or inferred.
- Priority and field-wide claims require coverage beyond a single paper.

## Compression

Keep details that change comparison, reproducibility, scope, mechanism, or practical use. Compress routine optimization and repeated background. When several studies form a development line, summarize the platform once and give each follow-up only the change it contributes.

## Citations

Place citations after the claim they support. Split a sentence when different sources support different clauses. A review source may orient the field; primary evidence supports experimental details and high-risk claims.
